Brain Navigator privacy policy (DRAFT)

Brain Navigator is a school planner and wellness companion for high school students. It is not a medical service, not therapy, and not for emergencies. It's for people 13 and older; if you're under 13, please don't use it.

1. The short version

  • Most of what you put in stays on your phone.
  • Some things are saved to our database so they're not lost (listed below). ⚠ Only your phone can read your copy.
  • AI is optional. Nothing goes to the AI until you agree on the screen that explains it, and you can take that back any time.
  • We don't sell your data, don't show ads, don't use tracking or analytics, and never use your health information for marketing.

3. The AI (Ember's "thinking brain" and the helpers)

Ember's replies can be written by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic. Ember is a computer program, not a person, and can't feel emotions. The first time you ask for an AI reply, the app shows exactly what would be sent and asks for your OK. Without it, nothing is sent and Ember uses its own built-in questions.

When you've agreed and you ask for a reply, the app sends: what you type in that chat (with phone numbers, emails and street addresses blanked out on your phone first), and a short note so the reply fits your day: your first name and grade, your classes, what's due this week, what's open on your Desk, today's check-in numbers (which can include sleep from Apple Health), and, only if you turn them on, About you and things Ember remembers. "Keep my Desk private" leaves the Desk and check-in out. You can hold any reply to see exactly what was sent.

It goes through our server to Anthropic, which writes the reply. Anthropic's commercial terms say it doesn't train its models on this data; it keeps it for a limited time for trust and safety [confirm current period with Anthropic's terms]. You can turn AI off everywhere, or take back your OK, in Settings → How AI works here.

4. Safety

If something you write sounds like you might be in danger, the app shows crisis lines right away, on your phone, before and without any AI: call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or text HOME to 741741 (Crisis Text Line), and the Need help now page. Ember's AI is instructed to respond with care, urge you to reach a real person, and never to give medical advice. In a chat, Ember reminds you that it's a computer program at the start and at least every three hours, with a nudge to take a break. These numbers are for the US.

5. Who else sees data

Only these service providers, only to run the app, and each bound to protect it at least as well as this policy: Supabase (database), Anthropic (AI, only with your OK), Open-Meteo (weather, approximate location), Apple (Health, speech, if you use them), Google and Instructure (Canvas), only if you connect them, and Notion only if you connect your own Notion workspace (your data goes to your workspace, not ours). We don't sell or share personal data for advertising, and we don't disclose health data to anyone except as listed here or as the law requires.

Crisis protocol (DRAFT)

Draft for lawyer review. California SB 243 asks operators of companion chatbots to publish how they handle suicidal ideation and self-harm; New York GBL Art. 47 asks for the same protocol. This describes what the app does today, in the code named beside each step. Publish it on the same page as the privacy policy once reviewed.

What happens

  1. Detection on the phone, before any AI. Every message typed or spoken to Ember or a helper, and what's written in Let it out, the check-in, the worry box and Put the day down, is checked on the phone against a list of phrases about suicide, self-harm, wanting to die, overdose, abuse or not being safe at home (soundsLikeCrisis, core/emberAI.ts). The list is tested against everyday school stress ("this test is killing me") so it doesn't cry wolf (tests/safety.test.ts).
  2. Help on screen at once. A match shows the help card right away, with or without a connection, with AI on or off, and whether or not the student has any AI replies left (HelpCard, components/ember/HelpCard.tsx): call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), text HOME to 741741 (Crisis Text Line), call 911 in an emergency, and the Need help now page (app/help.tsx).
  3. The AI is told what to do. Ember's instructions (SAFETY in supabase/functions/ember/index.ts) say: respond with warmth first, then clearly urge reaching a real person now (a trusted adult, 988, 741741); take it seriously every time, even as a joke; never give instructions or encouragement for self-harm, suicide, disordered eating or anything dangerous; never claim to be a therapist; never act as a romantic partner; say honestly that it's an AI.
  4. The server makes sure. If the student's newest message matches the list, the server adds the Need help now button to the reply even if the model didn't, and drops the suggested follow-up chips (same file, "Crisis protocol" comment). The phone and server lists are checked to be identical by the tests.
  5. If the AI declines or fails, Ember answers with a gentle line and the help button rather than an error (refusal handling in the function; offline fallback in the app).
  6. Reminders that it's an AI. Ember's chat says it's a computer program at the start, and again with a nudge to take a break every three hours of talking (core/companionRules.ts).

What the app doesn't do

  • It doesn't contact anyone on the student's behalf, alert parents, or call emergency services; it puts the numbers and people in front of the student.
  • It doesn't store which messages matched. [Lawyer: SB 243's annual report from July 1, 2027 asks for the number of crisis referrals; decide whether to keep an anonymous count, with no message text, to report it.]
  • It isn't monitored by people in real time.

Limits

The phrase list can miss things said indirectly, and can't judge tone. The help lines are US numbers. Ember is not a counselor; the app says so on the help page, in the consent sheet, in the terms, and in every AI chat.
